**🕒 17:01 👤 @trey.io (Trey Hunner)**
💬 Python Tip #264 (of 365):

Don't use the file readlines method. 🧵

Do this:
lines = list(file)

Not this:
lines = file.readlines()

Both give the same list of lines. So why prefer list?

#Python #DailyPythonTip

[Открыть пост](https://bsky.app/profile/trey.io/post/3mw26uhhred2y)

----
**🕒 17:01 👤 @trey.io (Trey Hunner)**
💬 Files are iterables of lines, and list(...) is THE way to say "loop over this iterable and store the results in a new list".

With readlines, it's not as obvious that we're making a list. If I didn't understand the history of readlines, I'd have assumed it returned an iterator (lots of things do).

[Открыть пост](https://bsky.app/profile/trey.io/post/3mw26uhm4yb2d)

----
**🕒 17:01 👤 @trey.io (Trey Hunner)**
💬 I'd argue that list(...) is also the modern way to get a list of lines from a file. The readlines method predates files being iterables and even iterators existing in Python in general.

But list() also generalizes: it works on files, generators, zip objects, and ANY other iterable.
